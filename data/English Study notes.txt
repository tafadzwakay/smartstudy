Comprehensive English Language Notes (Forms 1–4, based on Zimbabwe syllabus)
Overview
These notes are based on the Zimbabwe Forms 1–4 English Language syllabus. They are organized into the major learning areas: grammar, composition, comprehension, oral work, summary, registers, and exam practice. The notes are detailed and suitable for revision.
 
FORM 1–4 ENGLISH LANGUAGE
Chapter 1: Grammar (Language Structures)
Parts of Speech
Nouns
A noun is a naming word. Examples:
•	person: teacher
•	place: Harare
•	thing: book
•	idea: honesty Types:
•	common nouns
•	proper nouns
•	abstract nouns
•	collective nouns
•	concrete nouns
Pronouns
Used instead of nouns. Examples:
• he, she, they, it, we
Verbs
Action or state words. Examples:
•	run
•	speak
•	is
•	were
Adjectives
Describe nouns. Examples:
•	beautiful house
•	diligent student
Adverbs
Describe verbs/adjectives. Examples:
•	quickly
•	carefully
 
Tenses
Present Simple
Used for routines. Example: I go to school every day.
Past Simple
Used for completed actions. Example: She visited her grandmother.
Future Tense
Example: They will write exams.
Present Continuous
Example: He is reading.
Present Perfect
Example: She has completed her homework.
 
Subject-Verb Agreement
Singular subject takes singular verb. Plural subject takes plural verb.
Examples:
•	The boy runs.
•	The boys run.
 
Chapter 2: Composition Writing
Types of Composition
Narrative Composition
Tells a story. Structure:
•	introduction
•	rising action
•	climax
•	conclusion Example topics:
•	A day I will never forget
•	The accident I witnessed
Descriptive Composition
Describes people, places, or events. Use vivid language.
Example: Describe your school sports day.
Argumentative Composition
Presents two sides of an issue.
Example: Should mobile phones be allowed at school?
Discursive Composition Balanced discussion.
 
Composition Writing Tips
1.	Plan before writing.
2.	Use paragraphs.
3.	Use correct punctuation.
4.	Check spelling.
5.	End with a strong conclusion.
 
Chapter 3: Comprehension
What is Comprehension?
Reading a passage and answering questions.
Skills:
•	identifying main ideas
•	inference
•	vocabulary in context
•	summary
•	evaluating tone
Techniques
Skimming
Reading quickly for general idea.
Scanning
Searching for specific information.
Inference
Reading between the lines.
Example
Passage says: “The sky darkened and villagers rushed indoors.” Inference: a storm was approaching.
 
Chapter 4: Summary Writing
Rules
•	use own words
•	avoid examples unless essential
•	write only key points
•	use correct tense
•	observe word limit
Steps
1.	Read passage.
2.	Underline key points.
3.	Rewrite in concise form.
4.	Count words.

Chapter 11: Common Grammar Errors
Incorrect: He go to school. Correct: He goes to school.
Incorrect: I did not went. Correct: I did not go.
Incorrect: Their going home. Correct: They’re going home.
 
Chapter 12: Exam Practice
Composition Practice
Write about: 1. My first day at secondary school. 2. Should uniforms be abolished? 3. Describe a market scene.
Comprehension Practice
Read a passage and answer:
•	What is the main idea?
•	Explain the meaning of a word in context.
•	Infer the writer’s mood.
Grammar Practice
1.	Identify nouns and verbs.
2.	Change sentence into passive voice.
3.	Rewrite in reported speech.
 
Worked Examples
Example 1
Change to passive voice: The boy kicked the ball. Answer: The ball was kicked by the boy.
Example 2
Direct to reported speech: She said, “I am tired.” Answer: She said that she was tired.
Example 3
Correct the sentence: He don’t like books. Answer: He doesn’t like books.
 
Exam Tips
1.	Read instructions carefully.
2.	Plan compositions.
3.	Use paragraphs.
4.	Revise grammar daily.
5.	Practice oral reading aloud.
6.	Read novels and newspapers to improve vocabulary.
7.	Manage time in examinations.
